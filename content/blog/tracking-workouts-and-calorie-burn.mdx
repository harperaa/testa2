## Summary

Workouts are the most visible part of calories out — but they are not the whole story. Grok Fit helps you log activity, estimate burn, and compare training days with rest days so exercise supports your goal instead of justifying random eating.

## Why workout logging matters

Logging exercise does three jobs:

1. **Raises calories out** with a realistic estimate
2. **Documents training consistency** next to your nutrition log
3. **Prevents revenge eating** by showing the real size of a session burn

A 30-minute run might burn a few hundred calories. That is meaningful — and still easy to erase with an untracked snack.

## Strength vs cardio burns

| Session type | Typical focus | Logging tip |
|--------------|---------------|-------------|
| **Strength** | Muscle stimulus, lower continuous burn | Log duration + effort; don’t invent huge burn numbers |
| **Cardio / HIIT** | Higher continuous or interval burn | Log distance/time; compare week over week |
| **Steps / NEAT** | Daily movement outside the gym | Count walks and standing — they often beat one hard session |
| **Recovery** | Mobility, yoga, easy movement | Log them; keep burn modest |

Accuracy beats optimism. Overstating burn creates a false surplus and stalled fat-loss goals.

## Training days vs rest days

Your body still burns a lot on rest days through BMR and normal movement. Smart plans often:

- Keep **protein high every day**
- Slightly **increase carbs around hard training**
- Hold a **steadier deficit** rather than starving on rest days and bingeing after workouts

In Grok Fit, compare calories in vs out across a full week. A perfect training day followed by three unlogged rest days is still a missed week.

## A simple workout logging template

For each session, capture:

- **Name** — e.g. “Upper body strength” or “5K easy run”
- **Category** — Workout
- **Calories** — best estimate from your watch, machine, or a conservative formula
- **Focus** — Cardio, Strength, Recovery, or Steps
- **Status** — Logged when done, Pending if you will fill details later

That is enough structure for trends without turning training into admin work.

## Common workout-tracking traps

### 1. Double counting steps and dedicated cardio

If your watch already includes a run in daily burn, don’t add the same run twice as a huge bonus. Prefer one clear source of truth.

### 2. Using exercise to “earn” unlimited food

Energy balance still rules. Celebrate training with consistency, not with erasing the session in the kitchen.

### 3. Ignoring progressive overload

Burn numbers are useful, but strength progress also proves training quality. Pair calorie logs with lift or pace notes over time.

## How this ties to Grok Fit goals

- **Fat loss:** workouts raise calories out; keep intake controlled so the deficit stays real
- **Muscle gain:** training drives adaptation; a small surplus fuels recovery
- **Maintenance:** exercise quality and routine matter more than large swings in burn

Your dashboard chart of calories in vs out makes those strategies visible instead of theoretical.

## Bottom line

Track workouts honestly, count the full day of calories out, and let the weekly balance guide adjustments. Grok Fit keeps training and nutrition on the same screen so effort in the gym shows up in the plan.
