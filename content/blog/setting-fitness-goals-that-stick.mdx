## Summary

Vague goals like “get in shape” fail because they never connect to daily behavior. Strong goals name an outcome, a timeline, and a calorie strategy. Grok Fit turns that strategy into a daily energy target so every meal and workout has a job.

## Pick one primary goal

You can care about many things, but **one primary goal** should drive your calorie target:

- **Fat loss** — modest daily deficit, preserve protein and strength training
- **Muscle gain** — small surplus, progressive overload, enough protein
- **Maintenance** — balance intake and burn while building habits

Switching goals every week resets progress. Commit for at least 4–8 weeks unless health requires a change.

## Make the goal measurable

Bad: “Lose weight.”  
Better: “Lose 8–12 lb over 12 weeks while logging 6 days a week.”

Bad: “Get stronger.”  
Better: “Add 20 lb to my squat in 10 weeks while staying near maintenance calories.”

### Use process + outcome metrics

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Outcome** | Body weight, waist, lift PRs, race time |
| **Process** | Meals logged, workouts completed, protein target days |

Grok Fit shines on process metrics: streak of logged days, deficit adherence, workout burn consistency.

## Connect goals to calories

Once the outcome is clear, set energy rules:

1. Estimate maintenance (your average calories out over a normal week)
2. Apply a goal-based offset:
   - Fat loss: −300 to −500 kcal/day
   - Muscle gain: +200 to +300 kcal/day
   - Maintenance: ±100 kcal of average burn
3. Review weekly — not every morning after one heavy meal

Your dashboard should answer: *Did this week match my goal?*

## Habit design that survives busy weeks

### Default actions beat motivation

- Log breakfast before email
- Pre-log planned workouts on Sunday
- Keep 2–3 “default meals” you can enter in seconds
- Use pending entries for meals you will log after the fact — then clear them daily

### Recovery is part of the goal

Aggressive goals that ignore sleep, stress, and rest days collapse. Build **deload weeks** and maintenance phases into longer plans so you can stay consistent for months, not days.

## When to change the plan

Change the target when:

- Weight trend is flat for 2–3 weeks despite adherence
- Energy and training quality drop for more than a week
- Life stress makes the current deficit unsustainable
- You hit a mini-goal and want a planned maintenance break

Do **not** change the plan after one social weekend. Look at the weekly chart first.

## How Grok Fit keeps goals honest

- Goal progress cards show how far you are into the plan
- Net balance shows whether today supports fat loss, gain, or hold
- Logs capture the real behavior behind the goal
- Pro insights (when enabled) surface patterns you might miss

## Bottom line

A fitness goal that sticks is specific, time-bound, and tied to calories in and calories out. Set the goal, pick the energy strategy, and let Grok Fit show whether your days match the plan.
