## Summary

Energy balance is the foundation of every fitness goal. When calories in exceed calories out, you gain weight over time. When burn exceeds intake, you lose weight. Grok Fit tracks both sides so you can see your net balance every day — and adjust before the scale surprises you.

## What “calories in” really means

Calories in is everything you eat and drink that contains energy: meals, snacks, cooking oils, coffee creamers, and alcohol. Most people undercount here, not overcount.

### Practical ways to log intake

- Log meals as you go, not at night from memory
- Include oils, sauces, and drinks — they add up fast
- Use consistent portions so weekly averages stay honest
- Prefer whole-day accuracy over perfect single-food precision

## What “calories out” includes

Calories out is more than the workout. It includes:

- **Basal metabolic rate (BMR)** — energy your body uses at rest
- **Daily movement** — walking, standing, chores
- **Exercise** — runs, lifts, sports, classes
- **Digestion** — a smaller cost of processing food

Grok Fit combines resting burn with activity so your “out” number reflects a full day, not only gym time.

## Deficit, surplus, and maintenance

| Balance | What it means | Common goal |
|---------|---------------|-------------|
| **Deficit** | Out > In | Fat loss |
| **Surplus** | In > Out | Muscle gain |
| **Maintenance** | In ≈ Out | Hold weight, rebuild habits |

A sustainable fat-loss deficit is often about **300–500 kcal/day**, not extreme cuts. Muscle gain usually needs a **small surplus** plus progressive training. Maintenance is underrated — it is where many people rebuild consistency.

## How Grok Fit uses energy balance

1. You set a fitness goal (lose, gain, or maintain)
2. Grok Fit suggests a daily calorie target
3. You log food (in) and activity (out)
4. The dashboard shows net balance and weekly trends
5. You adjust portions or training when the trend drifts

That loop beats guessing. One high day is noise; a week of surplus or deficit is a signal.

## Common mistakes to avoid

### 1. Only tracking workouts

Ignoring food means you only see half the equation. A hard workout does not cancel an unlogged dinner.

### 2. Crash deficits

Very large deficits feel fast, then stall: hunger, fatigue, and missed training. Moderate, trackable deficits win long term.

### 3. Never reviewing the week

Daily numbers matter, but **weekly averages** tell the truth. Use Grok Fit’s charts to spot patterns, not just single days.

## A simple starting protocol

1. Pick one primary goal for the next 4–8 weeks
2. Log every meal and workout for 14 days without aggressive cuts
3. Review your average balance
4. Nudge intake or activity by 200–300 kcal toward your goal
5. Hold that plan for two weeks, then reassess
